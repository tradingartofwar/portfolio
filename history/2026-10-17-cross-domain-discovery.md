**Status:** Dated development account, with linked source records. This is a record of collaborative direction and proposals, not a claim that downstream applications have been validated.

## The trajectory

The morning began with a review of the Lonely Runner runner-addition study and its center-width generalization. The research formulated a rational-center condition using the required half-width H_d(c), core-permitted half-width G_{S,d}(c), and margin M(c)=G(c)-H(c). The next question is whether candidate centers can be selected or rejected directly from core constraints. The source work distinguishes bounded computations and same-author proof candidates from independent mathematical review.

Rather than treating a full solution of the Lonely Runner conjecture as the sole measure of value, Vance explicitly sought transferable value. The discussion moved from the mathematical distinction between required and permitted room to a *hypothesis* about agent coordination: individually adequate capabilities may not be jointly compatible. This is an analogy and research direction, not a proven transfer of the interval formula.

Vance suggested making agents do **actual executable software tasks requiring coordination**, then proposed building a controlled environment to regulate capabilities, information, permissions and communication. He also wanted to **see the interactions**, live or through replay. This produced the Agent Coordination Laboratory proposal and a subsequent bounded design review, with real software output, event records, integrity checks and experimental controls. No general agent-performance advantage has been demonstrated.

Vance then recognized a possible application to the Human–AI Network: connecting people not merely through common keywords or interests but through complementary capabilities and feasible collaboration. The question became what a connection might make possible, including problem-solving and employer/candidate discovery. These remain product and research hypotheses, with human consent and judgment essential.
