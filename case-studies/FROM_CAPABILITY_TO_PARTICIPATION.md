## The question was there in March

Vance adds a second question to the [March 2024 origin](HOW_WE_STARTED.md). By the end of that month, after the local chatbot build, he was already wondering what might become possible if human–AI partnerships could collaborate with one another.

The later network project therefore has an early intellectual starting point. Subsequent work and encounters gave the question more concrete requirements.

## From conversation to action

During online generative AI study in early 2025, Vance recalls hearing a need for a best-practices operating system for agents. In the first week, he wondered whether his chatbot could become agentic. By the following week, he recalls having built an agentic system intended to undertake a broad range of computer tasks.

This records his development account. The original runtime, exact date, and task coverage have not been recovered in this review. It does not establish that the system was among the first of its kind or could perform every task a human could perform on a computer. Formal course and credential details require their own verification.

He next describes building an office assistant in response to a practical workplace need. It could, in his account, answer the phone, consult schedules, make appointments, and pass relevant information to a therapist in another language.

The [Aichi account](AICHI_TRAJECTORIES.md) now includes his explanation of trajectory intelligence: recurring call paths, detours, and returns selected by a stateful controller, with Whisper and ElevenLabs in the voice system. He recalls natural-sounding, responsive operation hosted on his usual laptop. These are qualitative operational reports; the full local/cloud processing split and measured latency remain open.

He stopped developing the product because he expected general AI services to make this kind of assistance freely available. That was his product judgment at the time.

## Prototyping around NEXPLORE

Vance's account continues with a prototype-building GPT and digital lighting prototypes for human and plant needs, connected to his preparation for a space-focused gathering. He reports two provisional patent applications, one for terrestrial and one for extraterrestrial applications, and an eight-step video called *Pathway to Innovation*.

The event was **NEXPLORE 2040, September 9–11, 2025, at Amazon HQ2 in Arlington, Virginia**, according to [the organizer's event page](https://www.xfoundry.org/nexplore25/nexplore2025). Its program included a NASA fireside chat and work on AI beyond Earth. The invitation described below was personal; this record does not imply a formal NASA invitation or endorsement.

Vance identifies the person who invited him as a former MIT-course classmate whom he had helped with AI. He recalls that she was preparing one of the keynote presentations on humans and AI working together and thought he would be well suited to help. Ultimately, he says, the presentation shifted toward showcasing useful AI tools, and he did not take on that planned role. He still attended and describes it as a great event.

His prototype-building GPT was not used as he had expected. He describes the prototyping as a valuable learning experience. The precise order of the lighting prototypes, custom GPT, proposed competition, and video needs reconciliation with dated artifacts; the earlier account and this telling order some steps differently. His experience does not establish that the event itself held no competition.

The filings, prototype functions, and video have not been inspected here. “Provisional applications” is used deliberately: [USPTO guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/provisional-application) distinguishes these from granted patents. No present legal status, validated lighting performance, or external adoption is asserted.

## Meeting people without finding a path to contribute

Vance recalls being struck by the people and ideas at the event, including discussion of growing hearts in space. Yet after the first day he felt he was doing poorly at meeting people, and made a deliberate effort to improve.

One exchange stayed with him. He asked someone involved in lunar robotics about a superconducting approach that he thought might help with dust. When asked whether this was his field, he said it was an interest. He felt the conversation then closed before the possible contribution had been explored.

Vance clarified on October 16, 2026 that he had reached this idea independently and had not known about the NASA-hosted papers when he raised it. His thought was that supporting moving parts without mechanical contact could avoid the friction and interference caused by lunar dust. The connection to the published research was found while recording this story, rather than being the source of his suggestion at the event.

That is his experience of the encounter. The other person's motives and the engineering merits of the specific suggestion are not established. The term he was reaching for may be **flux pinning**, an editorial inference supported by the superconductivity literature noted below.

His broader observation was that interest in wider participation coexisted with limited practical opportunities to discover one another's interests and examine a contribution. He remembers thinking by the second day that a different networking arrangement was needed.

He envisioned AI helping people find shared questions, relevant experience, and complementary contributions, potentially without making conventional identity or status the first filter. This was a design direction, not an implemented anonymous matching service.

## The job search made the representation problem personal

Vance also recalls using an AI system to apply for approximately 800 jobs over the preceding year. He remembers one AI interview opportunity that was rescheduled, with no role resulting. The application history has not been audited; the number is his estimate.

He describes lacking the “compressed credential” that might carry his abilities through initial screening. He also recalls hearing similar unsuccessful-search accounts from people with degrees. These are his interpretation and reports he encountered; they do not establish why any employer rejected or did not respond to an application.

His reason for seeking a role matters:

> “I wanted to be on a team and contribute and participate in this frontier.”

This gives the network an employment branch. He wants a company or its authorized agent to be able to examine a rich record of work, development, evidence, and interests, and identify a reason for an interview. A concise introduction would remain connected to the detail needed to assess a particular fit.

The proposed benefit is a better opportunity to understand a person's relevant contribution. The current record and private network pilot do not demonstrate improved hiring outcomes, employer adoption, or a working recruitment system.

## What this changes in the present project

The current design brings several threads together: an early belief in collaborating partnerships; experience building useful systems; difficulty making contributions discoverable at a gathering; and difficulty representing capability through applications.

The living record supplies depth and routes to evidence. The network is intended to help people discover a shared question and choose work together. Career opportunities are one application of that wider purpose. The [current prototype account](FROM_INTRODUCTION_TO_COLLABORATION.md) shows the bounded implementation stage reached so far.
