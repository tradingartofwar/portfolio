## Problem and consequence

What decision or operation could fail? Which distinction changes the outcome?

## System or experiment

What is implemented? What is proposed? Specify inputs, outputs, trust boundary, and relevant authority.

## Evidence

Link exact source files and immutable revisions. Include commands, environment, dataset scope, counts, comparison method, and retained outputs. Distinguish source-reported tests from fresh execution.

## Result and correction

What happened? What failed or remained unknown? What evidence changed the interpretation?

## Limits

State missing controls, deployment limits, external-validity limits, independent-review status, and unverified claims.

## Contribution

Include one short decision episode where the record permits:

- Initial question or intended action.
- What the human noticed, authorized, redirected, rejected, or stopped.
- What AI proposed, implemented, checked, or interpreted.
- The source evidence and resulting change.

Identify whether each contribution is documented in a contemporaneous record, reported retrospectively, or inferred. Leave unsupported attribution unspecified. Do not infer unaided authorship from commits.

A real episode can show collaboration without proving a causal advantage over human-only or AI-only work. Preserve that distinction.

## Reproduce or challenge

Give the smallest useful reproduction path and evidence that could change the conclusion.

## Proposed relevance

Explain a potential contribution to research, engineering, or governance. Mark untested transfer explicitly.
